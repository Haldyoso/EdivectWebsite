This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The first screen is made only of words, colour and space, good enough that nobody misses a picture there. Pictures arrive later, large and few, as relief from the type. This is the honest answer when a subject has nothing worth photographing: instead of inventing illustrations, set what you have to say as well as it can be set. The typography carries a point of view through scale (one enormous statement, everything else small), through measure (a narrow, bookish column or a full-width line), and through one committed colour.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the statement, two lines at most, set at 9–14vw and occupying the full width with tight leading, positioned in the upper two-thirds. One supporting sentence at 20–24px sits bottom-left on the same margin and the single action sits bottom-right on the same baseline. No navigation bar; two or three links in a top corner. Sections that follow are typographic set pieces, each different: a list of five to eight capabilities set at 4–6vw one per line; a paragraph at 28–36px in a 28ch column pushed to the right half; a pair of large numerals with a sentence each; a single quotation filling a colour field. Each section uses a different horizontal position (left, right, full) so the page has movement without imagery.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. This direction lives or dies on the face and its settings. Display at tight tracking and leading; text sizes few and far apart (for example 15, 22, 34 and the display size). Use real typographic detail: proper quotes, balanced line breaks, hung punctuation where it helps.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Either one saturated ground for the whole page with a single ink, or paper and ink with one section inverted and one in the accent as a full field. Colour does the job images would have done.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected) by the app-screen rules (references/app-screens.md: deal its style once with `node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --app --kind <kind> --keep palette,type --out .taste-skill/app`, and build it with assets/app.css and assets/charts.js), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node C:/Dev/GitHub/EdivectWebsite/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

The first screen has no picture. Further down, real pictures of the subject's world (found with scripts/assets.mjs) arrive large and few: a full-bleed photograph between two type sections, a looping clip in one band, an archival image beside a list. Do not add icons, shapes, patterns or abstract graphics to compensate for anything.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — One word, cropped
A single word is the whole composition, set so large that the viewport crops it. No image. The word is one line in a heavy condensed or heavy grotesk face at clamp(360px, 46vw, 760px), line-height 0.8, white-space: nowrap, placed so its capitals fill the lower half of the screen: the baseline sits 6% below the bottom edge so the feet of the letters are cut, and the line is shifted 8vw to the left so the first letter is cut by the left edge and the last runs off the right (overflow: hidden on the section). Raise the size until both end letters are cut; three to five letters stay fully visible and the word still reads. The top 30% holds everything else on 28px margins: the wordmark at 16px top-left, three text links top-right, and, starting 96px down on the left margin, one supporting sentence at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), max 34ch, with the single action directly beneath it as a solid rectangle. Ground and ink are two flat values; the word may take the palette's one accent instead of the ink.

On a phone: The word turns a quarter turn (writing-mode: vertical-rl) and runs down the right side at 62vw, a fifth of its height cut by the right edge and its ends cut by the top and bottom. The left 45% of the screen holds the wordmark, the sentence at 17px and the action, stacked from the top on a 20px margin. Links fold behind a menu word.
Headline: One word of 4-8 letters, the name or the single noun the subject owns; it is cropped but must stay readable.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. showcase — Headline you can type (statement)
The section's headline is an input. One line at clamp(48px, 8vw, 132px), tight tracking, flush left across columns 1–12, is a real `<input>` (root marked data-ui) with no border and no fill, prefilled with a believable two-to-five-word example of what a user gives the product: a search, a question, a date in words, a colour name. A solid caret block 0.08em wide blinks after the last character. 48px beneath, on the same left edge, the product's answer appears at true interface size, at most 560px wide: three result rows, one parsed value, or a swatch, updating as the visitor types. Bottom-left, three more examples at 17px act as plain text buttons that refill the line. The rest of the 88svh field is empty. If the text outgrows the line it scrolls inside the input; it never wraps.
On a phone: The input is set at 11vw on one line and scrolls sideways inside itself when the text runs long. The answer follows 28px below at full width and true size. The three examples become a wrapping row of filled blocks 44px tall beneath the answer; tapping one refills the line.

### 2. how-it-works — Itinerary on a slip (table)
The process is printed as an itinerary or receipt. One paper-coloured slip 420–480px wide stands on a contrasting flat ground at columns 7–11; its bottom edge is a zigzag cut with clip-path and its top edge is straight. The heading, two to four words at clamp(40px, 4.4vw, 68px), sits in columns 1–5 with a 30ch paragraph, top-aligned with the slip. On the slip, in the text face at 16px with tabular figures: stages grouped under time headings that carry the order ('Monday', 'Midweek', 'Friday'), each heading in bold with 28px above it. Under a heading, two to four rows; each row has what happens on the left, a duration or time on the right, and a leader of round 4px dots between. The final row is inverted, paper-coloured text on a full-width ink bar 44px tall, and states the result: what the reader holds at the end and the total time.
On a phone: The heading and paragraph come first. The slip follows at full width minus 16px margins, keeping its zigzag bottom edge. Rows keep left text, dots and right value on one line; long descriptions wrap under the left text. The inverted final row stays full width.

### 3. features — Sentences with switches (band)
Four or five capabilities, each one plain sentence of five to eight words about what the product will do for you, each ending in a working switch. Every sentence is a full-bleed band 20vh tall, butted against the next with no gap. Inside, on the 12-column grid: the sentence at clamp(30px, 3.6vw, 60px) on one line in columns 1–10, vertically centred; in columns 11–12, right-aligned, the product's real toggle, built in em with its root marked data-ui, 1.1em tall so it matches the type. Off, a band is the page ground and its sentence is in the soft ink. On, the band fills with a palette colour, a different one per band, the sentence turns to full ink and a second line at 17px appears beneath stating the consequence in figures. Two bands are on at load. Toggles answer click, Space and Enter. There is no heading; the first sentence does that work.
On a phone: Bands are at least 132px tall. The sentence is set at 6.4vw on two lines in the left 76% of the width; the toggle sits at the right, vertically centred, with a 44px touch target. When a band is on, the consequence line opens under the sentence at 15px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 4. proof — Recognition as a table (table)
Awards, press mentions or credits set as a four-column table with no lines. The heading is two or three words at about 5vw, stepped over two lines with the second line indented by two columns, and occupies columns 1–7. The table begins 72px below and spans columns 1–12 on a fixed grid: year in columns 1–2, project or title in 3–6, awarding body or publication in 7–10, the award itself in 11–12, right-aligned. Rows are set at 16–17px with 14px of padding above and below; every second row carries a background tone one step from the ground, running the full table width, which replaces rules. Year groups are separated by one blank row, and the year is printed only on the first row of its group. Use a real `<table>` with `border-collapse: collapse` and no borders.
On a phone: The heading drops to 11vw. Each table row becomes a two-line block: title on the first line in the full ink, then body and award on the second in the softer ink. The year stands once as a 22px figure above its group. Alternate blocks keep the background tone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 5. list — Two-ended rows, narrow column (list)
A list in one narrow column, 520px wide, starting at 22% of the viewport width or centred. Every row is 34–38px tall with no box and nothing drawn between rows. Each row has two ends: at the left, a 16px round image (a face, a logo, a colour dot) followed after 8px by the name at 15–16px in full ink; at the far right of the column, a short value at 13px in the soft ink (a date, a price, a domain, an opening time) with tabular figures. Entries that need a sentence run to two lines at the left, and the value aligns with the first line. Groups are separated by 40px of space and begin with one plain word in the soft ink at the same size. A heading of two to four words at 24–28px in medium weight sits 32px above the first row. Holds 10–40 rows.
On a phone: The column fills the width with 20px margins. Rows are 44px tall for touch; the left image stays 16px. Long values wrap beneath the name at 13px, left-aligned with it, and the row grows. Group spacing is 32px.

### 6. faq — Large questions, answers beside (table)
Every answer is open; nothing collapses. Each question-and-answer pair is a row on the 12-column grid: the question in columns 1–6 at clamp(26px, 2.6vw, 40px), medium weight, leading 1.1, no more than two lines; its answer in columns 8–12 at 17–18px with leading 1.55 and a measure near 40ch, its first baseline level with the question's first baseline. Rows are separated by 72–88px of empty space and nothing else. Five to seven rows. The section heading, two or three words, is set at the same size as the questions but in the softer ink and sits as the first row's left cell with an empty right cell, so it reads as the top of the same table rather than a title above it. Because questions are large and answers small, the left column works as a scannable index.
On a phone: Each pair stacks: question at 24px, answer 12px beneath at 17px, then 56px of space before the next. The heading leads at the same 24px in the softer ink. Answers stay open; there is no accordion on the phone either.

### 7. cta — One word is the button (band)
The entire band is one link. A full-bleed field in the accent colour, about 60svh tall, contains a single verb of four to eight letters set at 24–30vw in the heaviest weight, tight tracking, sized with `clamp()` so it spans the content width on one line with its baseline 24px above the bottom edge. The `<a>` wraps the whole band; on hover and focus the field and the word swap colours instantly. In the top-left corner, at 20px, one sentence says what happens next; in the top-right, an arrow drawn as a simple inline SVG, 48px, points up and right. There is no other button anywhere in the section. In a page with a motif, the band's top corners take the motif's shape (a large radius, or a chamfer cut with `clip-path`). Keep the word honest: the action itself, not a slogan.
On a phone: The band is 46svh tall. The word is set at whatever size fills the width on one line; choose a shorter verb rather than going below 22vw. Sentence top-left at 17px across two lines, arrow top-right at 32px. The whole band stays tappable.

### 8. footer — Columns above, wordmark below (stack)
The footer inverts ground and ink and fills a whole viewport (min-height 100svh), arranged as three rows in a grid (auto 1fr auto). Top row, 40px from the edge: four small columns on the 12-column grid (address, navigation, social, hours or contact), each a plain list at 15–16px with its first line in the full ink and the rest in the softer ink. Middle row: empty. Bottom row: the wordmark alone, sized to run from the left margin to the right margin on one line. Set it as inline SVG text with a `viewBox` fitted to the word and `width: 100%`, or tune `font-size` in `vw` to the letter count, so it never wraps; its baseline sits 20px above a single line of legal text at 13px. With `position: sticky; bottom: 0` and a lower `z-index` than the page, the last section slides away to uncover it.
On a phone: Columns become two by two at 15px with 32px between rows, at the top. A gap of about 20svh follows. The wordmark spans the full width at the bottom; a name longer than ten letters stacks on two lines, each fitted to the width. Legal line beneath at 12px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

## 8. Motion — Scaling wordmark
The giant name responds to the scroll: as the first screen leaves, the wordmark scales down a few percent (or grows from 0.94 to 1 as the footer arrives) on a scroll-linked timeline, so the monument feels heavy. Travel stays under 8%. Static fallback where scroll timelines are unsupported. Recipe: 'Scroll-linked progress' in references/motion.md, animating `scale`.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Scale contrast and colour mass. A capability list set at 5vw is an image in its own right.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Without pictures, weak copy has nowhere to hide: write real sentences. Vary the set pieces; four sections of big-heading-plus-paragraph is a text document, not a design. Keep all headings within two lines.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
