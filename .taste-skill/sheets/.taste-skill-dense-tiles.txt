This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The whole page is a board of tiles fitted edge to edge with narrow gaps, like a tidy desk seen from above. Each tile is one thing: the name, a statement, a photograph, a number, a link, a short list. There is no hero and no sequence of bands; the page is one continuous composition that the eye roams. It suits subjects with many small true things to show (a studio with work, writing, clients and news; a product with several distinct capabilities) and it replaces the generic bento by having real content in every tile and a strict system for sizes.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with 8px gaps and 8–12px outer margins, filled completely, no empty cells. Tiles use only four sizes (for example 6×4, 6×2, 3×2, 3×4 in column × row units of about 90px). Every tile is a flat filled rectangle: ground colour one step from the page, or a palette colour, or a photograph covering it. One radius everywhere (0, or 6–10px). The first screen contains: a name tile (the name at display size, the largest type on the page) with the wordmark set large, a statement tile with the description at 28–40px, two or three image tiles and one action tile in the accent colour. Text inside tiles is top-left aligned with 20–24px padding and never centred. Below the first screen the board continues with the same sizes; the last row is the footer made of tiles.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. One grotesk in two weights. Inside tiles: a large size for single statements, a small size for lists. No uppercase labels in tile corners.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Page ground, tile ground one step lighter or darker, and one or two palette colours on selected tiles. Photographs supply the rest.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected) by the app-screen rules (references/app-screens.md: deal its style once with `node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --app --kind <kind> --keep palette,type --out .taste-skill/app`, and build it with assets/app.css and assets/charts.js), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node C:/Dev/GitHub/EdivectWebsite/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

Photographs fill whole tiles with `object-fit: cover`; choose images that crop well to both wide and tall tiles.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

Optional atmosphere, two at most, from the "Architecture" set (structure, lines, bridges, facades and building interiors). The page does not need them.

## 6. Hero — Sheet with a text cell
The whole viewport is a sheet of photographs: six columns with 12px gaps and 12px outer margins, each column a vertical stack of frames at natural ratios with square corners. Each column starts at a different vertical offset (0, -90, -40, -130, -20, -70px) so rows never line up and the top and bottom frames are cropped by the viewport. Every frame has a flat strip 28px tall fused to its bottom edge in a solid palette colour, carrying one line of 13px text (a title or a place); strip colours rotate through four values. There is no header. In columns 3-4, starting at 28% of the height, one cell of the sheet is not a photograph but a filled panel in the tone opposite to the ground, 410px tall, sitting on the grid like any other frame with the same gaps on all sides. Inside it, top-left aligned on 24px padding: the wordmark at 28px, one sentence at 20px on up to three lines, the single action as a filled rectangle, and three text links on one line at the panel's foot.

On a phone: The text panel spans the full width as the first block, about 40% of the screen height, with the wordmark at 7vw, the sentence at 17px and the action full width. Under it the sheet resumes in two columns with 8px gaps, the right column started 60px higher than the left, colour strips kept on every frame. The links sit in one row at the panel's foot.
Headline: The wordmark at 28px plus one sentence of up to 70 characters inside the panel; nothing is set at display size.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. showcase — Live demo with controls (split)
One filled panel, 680–760px wide and about 640px tall, radius 12px, in a tone clearly darker or lighter than the ground, placed on the text column's left edge or centred, with at least 15vh of empty ground above and below. The panel is split 62/38 into two flat areas that differ by one tone. Left area: the product doing its job on real content at true size. Right area: a narrow column of working controls, six to nine stacked rows 44px tall, each with a name at 14px on the left and a slider, toggle or value on the right; changing one changes the demonstration at once. A text link sits in the panel's bottom-left corner. The heading, two to four words at 24–32px, sits above the panel on its left edge with one sentence at 16px; under the panel, one line of start instruction.
On a phone: The panel fills the width. The demonstration takes the top at 4:3; the controls follow beneath as full-width rows 52px tall, only the four most telling ones, with a text link that reveals the rest. Heading and sentence sit above at 24px and 16px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.

### 2. how-it-works — Accordion of tinted panels (strip)
A two-column introduction, then one horizontal accordion. Introduction: the heading, two to four words at clamp(36px, 4vw, 60px), in columns 1–5; one paragraph at 18px in columns 7–11. 64px below, a flex row across columns 1–12, 520px tall, of four to six panels with no gaps. The open stage is a panel taking about 58% of the width; the rest are vertical slivers sharing what remains. Order is carried by colour: the first stage is the darkest tone of one hue and each later stage is a lighter flat tint of it, so the row reads dark to light from left to right. A sliver shows only its stage name, rotated 90 degrees and reading upward, at 20px bold, anchored to its bottom-left. The open panel shows the name at 40px top-left, a 36ch paragraph at 18px bottom-left and, optionally, one photograph in its right half. Clicking a sliver opens it (flex-basis transition, 450ms).
On a phone: The accordion turns vertical: stages stack at full width as bars 64px tall in the same dark-to-light order, names set horizontally at 18px. The open stage expands to its content height with the paragraph at 17px. The first stage is open by default.

### 3. features — Staggered fragment cells (grid)
A three-column grid of six filled cells with 8px gaps, each cell 420–460px tall and filled one tone off the ground, no outlines. Every cell has two parts: a text part (a title at 17px medium and three lines at 15px in the soft ink, 24px padding) and a real fragment of the interface at true size (a control, a menu, a table slice, a swatch strip) docked flush against the opposite edge of the cell and cropped by it. The stagger is the idea: across the first row cells run text-top, fragment-top, text-top; the second row runs the reverse, so fragments form a checkerboard. Fragments differ in shape and size on purpose. The heading, two to four words at 32–40px, sits left-aligned above the grid with 56px beneath it and no supporting line.
On a phone: One column of cells at their natural height with 6px gaps. Every cell runs text first, fragment second, but fragments alternate between docking to the left edge and the right edge, and they are allowed to run off the side of the cell at true size instead of shrinking.

### 4. proof — Large marks on tiles (grid)
A board of filled tiles, four across and two or three rows deep, with 8px gaps, spanning the full content width. Each tile is about 3:2, one step lighter or darker than the page ground, with no border. A tile holds one supplied logo, shown far larger than a usual logo strip: about 72px tall, centred, in the page ink as a single-colour SVG. When no logo files exist, the tile holds the name instead, set at 28–32px bold, top-left with 24px padding; never draw a mark. One tile, twice the width of the others and placed first, is in the accent colour and carries the heading: two to four words at about 3vw, top-left. Tile corners follow the page's one radius, or the page's motif shape if it has one. Tiles sit still; there is no hover lift.
On a phone: Two tiles per row with 6px gaps, logos at 44px tall. The heading tile runs full width first, about 120px tall. Show eight tiles at most so the board stays under one and a half screens. Names set in type drop to 20px.

### 5. list — Day tabs, banded agenda (list)
A two-line heading at clamp(36px, 4vw, 60px), first line in the soft ink and second in full ink, with the booking button on the same row at the far right. 56px below, left: a 150px column of stacked day tabs, each a filled block 150 by 64px with 6px gaps showing the weekday at 16px and the date at 13px; the active tab takes the darkest fill with inverted text. Right, from 190px to the right margin: the day's sessions as alternating tone bands, each 76px tall with an 8px radius and 4px gaps, the two tones one step apart. Inside a band, on fixed column starts: the time at 20px with tabular figures, 20px from the left; the title at 18px in medium weight at 22%, with a one-line note at 13px in the soft ink under it; speaker names right-aligned at 14px beside up to three overlapping 32px round portraits.
On a phone: The heading stacks above a full-width button. Day tabs become a horizontal row of equal filled blocks, sticky under the top edge. Each band stacks time over title over speakers with 16px padding and grows to fit; portraits sit at the band's top-right corner.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.

### 6. faq — Accordion of filled tiles (stack)
A two-part row. Columns 1–4 hold the heading, two to four words at about 4vw, `position: sticky; top: 96px` so it stays while the list scrolls past. Columns 5–12 hold the accordion: each question is a filled tile one step from the page ground, with 8px between tiles, no border and the page's one radius (or its motif's corner). A closed tile is 72px tall with the question at 20px medium on the left, 24px padding, and on the right a plus sign drawn from two 3px bars 16px long. The open tile switches to the accent colour with its assigned ink, the plus becomes a minus by rotating one bar, and the answer appears at 17px, no wider than 52ch. Build each tile as `<details>` with a shared `name` so one opens at a time. Six to nine tiles.
On a phone: The heading sits above at 9vw, not sticky. Tiles run full width with 6px gaps, questions at 18px wrapping to two lines, closed height at least 64px. The open tile keeps the accent colour, and answers are set at 16px with 20px padding.

### 7. cta — The action tile (grid)
The close is one row of the board. Three filled tiles sit side by side with 8–14px gaps, all 360px tall and sharing one radius: a wide centre tile spanning columns 3–10 in the accent colour, flanked by two narrow tiles (columns 1–2 and 11–12) that run off the left and right viewport edges, as if the row had been cropped from a longer one. The centre tile holds, left-aligned and vertically centred with 40px padding: a heading of three to five words at 40px, two lines at 17px, and the action. The action is either one rectangular button or an email field 420px wide in the paper colour with the submit button nested inside its right end. The flanking tiles hold no text: one is a photograph covering the tile, the other a flat palette colour. No shadows, no outlines.
On a phone: The accent tile runs full width, about 300px tall, heading at 28px, field and button stacked at full width. The two flanking tiles follow beneath as a pair, each half width and 120px tall, with the same gap and the same radius.

Adjusted for this page:
- It mentions a shadow: only on something open above the page (a menu, a dialog), never on a resting element.

### 8. footer — The mark, built large (band)
On a flat colour field, the brand closes the page as a construction rather than as set type. Across the full content width and about 34svh tall, the wordmark is built from blocks: each letter is knocked out of its own solid rectangle in a palette colour, the rectangles differ in height (60–100% of the band) and butt together with 4px gaps, bottoms on one line. Where the project has a simple geometric mark or motif, enlarge that instead, drawn as inline SVG from flat rectangles, circles and quarter-arcs with the same 4px gaps. Below, 56px lower: four columns of plain links at 15px across columns 1–8, and two addresses or contact lines in columns 9–12. A last row carries the copyright at the left and one small filled button at the right. Space and the block construction do all the separating.
On a phone: The block wordmark spans the full width at about 22svh tall; a long name breaks into two rows of blocks. Link columns go two across at 15px, contact lines beneath at full width, then copyright and the small button on one row.

## 8. Motion — Shutter image
The one hero or showcase image opens from an edge with a clip-path over about 1.1 seconds while the picture inside settles from 1.12 scale to rest. Use it once, on the image that matters most; later images simply are there. Recipe: 'Image shutter' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The fit. A full board with consistent gaps and varied tile contents is satisfying in the way a packed suitcase is.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Tiles are filled, never outlined, and never carry shadows. Do not put an icon and a two-line feature blurb in every tile; vary what tiles hold. Decide the phone layout as its own board: two columns, tiles re-ordered so the name and statement lead.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
